Did you know that a single thunderstorm can turn the Grand Canyon into a deadly trap in a matter of minutes?

In August 2022, intense monsoon rains triggered a massive debris flow at Tatahatso Beach. Rafters watched in horror as a wall of mud and "fridge-sized rocks" thundered
down, destroying camps for eight straight hours.

Miraculously, everyone survived that day. But just one year earlier at the exact same beach, a similar storm dumped nearly two inches of rain in just 40 minutes. A 400-foot waterfall of mud and rock caught 32 rafters, tragically claiming one life.

It keeps happening. In August 2024, a flash flood at Havasu Creek took another life and forced the National Guard to airlift over 200 stranded tourists.

And just last month, in August 2026, severe cloudbursts sent a 15-foot wall of debris tearing through Bright Angel Canyon, killing at least two people and wiping out the park’s main water pipeline.

In the canyon, dry ground can become a raging torrent in seconds. Always check the weather, and never underestimate the power of a desert monsoon.
